About us

A real working farm that opened its gates

Hollow Creek started as a livestock operation — not a tourist attraction. We raise animals, tend the land, and run a working farmstead outside Chattanooga.

We opened to visitors because we believe people deserve to know what a real farm looks and feels like. Every animal you meet, every row you walk through — it's all part of an operation that runs 365 days a year.

Our story

How Hollow Creek came to be

The farmstead began the way most small farms do — with a piece of land, a lot of work, and a vision for what a self-sustaining operation could look like. We built the livestock side first, learning as we went, making mistakes, and figuring out what it actually takes to run animals well across all four seasons.

The agritourism idea came later. Neighbors started asking if they could bring their kids out to see the animals. Friends wanted to pick apples. Word spread. Eventually it became clear that opening the farm up wasn't a distraction from the real work — it was an extension of it.

We built the visitor side of the farm the same way we built everything else: practically, without pretension, and with the goal of making something that actually lasts.

What we believe

Why we do it this way

Real over curated
The mud is real. The animals are real. The work is real. We don't tidy things up — this is what a farm actually looks like.
Families first
Everything we've designed — the pricing, the layout, the wagon timing — is built around families with young kids having a real, unhurried day.
Fair pricing
A $20 all inclusive pass that covers everything is a deliberate choice. We set out to be the farm people come back to because it doesn't cost a fortune.
Seasonal honesty
We only offer what's genuinely in season. When the flowers are done, they're done. We'd rather be honest about that than stretch things past their time.
Common questions

A bit more about the farm

Is this a real working farm or just an attraction?
Both — but the working farm came first. The livestock operation runs all year. The agritourism side is open April through October. When you visit, the animals you see are the same ones that live here in January.
Do you grow everything on site?
Yes. The apple orchard, pumpkin patch, and flower fields are all grown and tended on our land. What you pick is what we grew.
Are you family-owned?
Yes, entirely. Hollow Creek is privately owned and operated. There's no franchise, no corporate backing — just people who wanted to build something worth coming back to.
Can I visit outside of agritourism season?
The farm itself operates year-round, but we're only open to visitors April through October. Follow us on social media for off-season updates and early access to season pass sales.

Come see it for yourself

Open April through October, just outside Chattanooga. Walk in free or grab a $20 all inclusive pass and stay all day.
